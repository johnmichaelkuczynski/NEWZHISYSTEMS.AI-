🧭 The Eight-Topic Journey
1. The Dual Operating System Model
Begin with the course’s organizing idea: a person may move between a more reflective adult or mature mode and an earlier, developmentally fixed mode. Learn why this is a conceptual model—not a claim that a person literally contains two minds.

2. Fixation and Trauma
Explore how development may become organized around unmet needs, overwhelming experiences, repetition, and adaptation. Distinguish a psychodynamic formulation from a simplistic claim that one event explains every feature of BPD.

3. Splitting vs. Repression
Examine two different ways difficult experience may be managed. Learn why keeping contradictory self-and-other experiences apart is not the same as pushing a conflict out of awareness.

4. Oscillation Between States
Study rapid shifts in feeling, expectation, memory, attachment, and action. Consider how changing internal states can make the same relationship feel radically different from one moment to the next.

5. Adult and Mature Capacities
Identify the capacities that support reflection, reciprocity, delay, complexity, responsibility, and the ability to hold mixed feelings without collapsing into an all-good or all-bad view.

6. Infantile and Fixated States
Understand developmentally earlier needs and defenses without using “infantile” as an insult. The emphasis is on states of experience, not on reducing an adult person to a childish stereotype.

7. BPD vs. Psychopathy
Work through careful distinctions involving attachment, empathy, guilt, aggression, manipulation, emotional reactivity, and personality organization. Avoid sensationalism and learn why overlapping behavior does not make two conditions identical.

8. Clinical Implications
Bring the model into the helping relationship. Explore transference, countertransference, boundaries, rupture and repair, diagnostic humility, and why psychotherapy remains central while medication is generally adjunctive and symptom-targeted.
