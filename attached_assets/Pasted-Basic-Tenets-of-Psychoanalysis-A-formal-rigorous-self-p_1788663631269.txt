Basic Tenets of Psychoanalysis
A formal, rigorous, self-paced course for adult learners entering psychoanalytic theory, history, technique, and debates about evidence.

Curriculum
The curriculum contains exactly thirty course topics:

The unconscious: most of the mind is hidden from the mind
Psychic determinism: nothing mental is accidental
Repression: the mind actively keeps knowledge from itself
Drives: wish, want, and the engine of mental life
The pleasure principle and the reality principle
Id, ego, superego: the structural model
Conscious, preconscious, unconscious: the topographic model
Infantile sexuality and the stages of development
The Oedipus complex and the formation of conscience
Fixation and regression
Defense mechanisms: denial, projection, reaction formation, sublimation, rationalization
Symptom formation: the symptom as compromise
Dreams as wish fulfillment: manifest content and latent content
Dream work: condensation, displacement, symbolization, secondary revision
Parapraxes: slips, errors, and forgetting as evidence
Free association and the fundamental rule
Transference: the past replayed on the analyst
Countertransference
Resistance and working through
Interpretation and insight as the mechanism of cure
Anxiety: signal anxiety and its sources
Narcissism and object relations
Mourning, melancholia, and identification
Ambivalence and the conflict of opposed wishes
The death drive and repetition compulsion
Civilization and the cost of instinctual renunciation
The unconscious in everyday life: jokes, art, and religion
Post-Freudian revisions: Jung, Adler, Klein, Lacan
The scientific status of psychoanalysis: evidence, testability, critique
Psychoanalysis and the modern mind sciences
Each lecture is available at Short, Medium, and Long depth. The treatment is historically informed and conceptually exacting: it distinguishes observation from interpretation, clinical pattern from metapsychological hypothesis, and therapeutic outcome from evidence for a proposed mechanism.

Learning and assessment
Section-scoped AI tutoring grounded in the lecture being read
Adaptive practice by topic and persistent difficulty
Two homework sets, a timed course test, and a cumulative final
Concrete, multi-sentence case analysis rather than definition recall
Subject and General Reasoning diagnostics in multiple formats, lengths, and phases
Semantic grading with per-problem rationale
Static and diachronic keystroke-pattern AI-authorship screening
Public PDF and TXT course readers with no account required
Analytics for assignment performance and topic mastery
All freeform answer and tutor inputs retain the existing math keyboard. Routes, public access, API data shapes, grading mechanics, and diagnostic mechanics remain unchanged.

Technical architecture
Contract-first Express API with OpenAPI-generated Zod validators and React Query hooks
PostgreSQL and Drizzle for curriculum, attempts, practice, diagnostics, and analytics
Server-Sent Events and model-backed tutoring/generation
Transactional curriculum reseeding through a versioned seed_meta marker
GPTZero-backed static detection with non-blocking fallbacks, plus keystroke-trace analysis
The seed contains thirty curriculum topics plus two diagnostic-primer records. The system diagnostic verifies the fixed thirty-topic curriculum count.
