Edgington on Conditionals: Confusing a Statement with a Number
Edgington's view, set out in "On Conditionals" (Mind, 1995), is that "if A, then B" does not express a proposition and has no truth conditions. What it has instead is a conditional probability: the value of the conditional just is P(B given A), your degree of belief in B on the supposition of A. She holds this because of a triviality result — Lewis proved that no proposition's probability of being true can equal P(B given A) across the board, so anyone who insists that the conditional's value is the conditional probability must give up the claim that conditionals are true or false. Edgington keeps the probability identity and throws out truth. The result is that "if Smith is a whale, then Smith is a mammal" — a flatly, analytically true statement — cannot be true on her view. It can only be a credence of 1. She gets the right number by denying there is a fact for the number to be about.
The view is shit for a reason that requires no logic at all, only attention to grammar. "If A, then B" is a statement — the kind of thing that asserts something is the case and is true or false. P(B given A) is a number — a magnitude, a noun phrase, the kind of thing that is high or low, .9 or .3. You cannot identify a statement with a number; they are not the same category of thing, so no equation between them can be literally true. At most the number measures something about the statement — how confident you are in it — which already presupposes the statement as something there to be confident about. So the identity she protects was malformed from the start, and Lewis's triviality result is not a deep discovery about the hidden nature of conditionals; it is just the noise the error makes when you try to run it. Kill the type confusion and there is no triviality to accommodate, because there was never a coherent identity for the proof to operate on. Edgington spent the foundational move of her career mistaking a measurement of a statement for the statement, and built everything downstream on the mistake.
