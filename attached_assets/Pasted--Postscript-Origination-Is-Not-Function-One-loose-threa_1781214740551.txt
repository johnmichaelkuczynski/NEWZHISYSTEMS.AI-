# Postscript: Origination Is Not Function

One loose thread remains, and pulling it tight finishes the case. The capstone conceded an apparent vulnerability: cells were once free-living individuals, so the "individual" is sometimes temporally prior to the collective even where the collective is now explanatorily prior. I treated that evolutionary fact as a genuine counterweight the thesis had to concede ground to. It isn't, and seeing why removes the last foothold a critic had.

The concession smuggles in a category confusion between two different axes: *origination* and *function*. A fact about where a part came from has no purchase on a claim about what the part now is and how it must be understood. Yes, cells originated as free-living organisms more than a billion years ago — and so what? The cell *in the fox*, the cell *in the octopus*, the cell *in you* is not that ancestor and is not intelligible as one. It is a neuron, a myocyte, a liver cell, and each is defined exhaustively by its office within the organism. Try to understand a heart-muscle cell "on its own terms," as a sovereign survival-unit, and you grasp almost nothing about why it does what it does. Understand it as the heart's instrument and its entire behavior snaps into focus. The origination axis tells you about ancestry; the function axis tells you about being. They do not trade against each other, so the evolutionary "concession" was never a concession at all — it was an answer to a different question than the one being asked.

Once the axes are separated, the strong version of the thesis is simply true, with nothing left to qualify. The claim was never that collectives come *first in time* — that is the weak, contestable formulation. The claim is that the part is *now* constituted by its role in the whole and is intelligible only through it — which holds regardless of origin, exactly as the meaning of a word in a sentence is untouched by the etymology of where the word came from. So the human case carries over without remainder. It does not matter whether one imagines humanity descended from some notionally pre-social ancestor, because the human *as actually encountered* — conformist, custom-deferring, norm-enforcing, status-seeking — is constituted by membership the way the muscle cell is constituted by the heart.

And this exposes Hobbes's error in its final and most exact form. His "individual in the state of nature" is precisely the mistake of taking the free-living ancestor cell as the model for the cell-in-the-organism. He reached back along the origination axis, seized a hypothetical solitary unit, and tried to explain *present function* with it. That is not a rival theory of society; it is an axis confusion. The free-living protozoan is no guide to the neuron, and the imagined solitary appetite-maximizer is no guide to the person — and in both cases for the identical reason: how a thing began is not what a thing is.
