# The Asymmetry of Explanation and Prediction, Done Correctly

Explanation and prediction are not symmetrical. Everyone serious agrees on that much. The question is *why*, and the right answer is simple, decisive, and owes nothing to any theory of science. The wrong answer is Popper's.

Here is the correct analysis. To predict correctly it is not enough to know current conditions plus the operative mechanisms. You must also know that those mechanisms will not be overridden by other, stronger ones—and that is a negative piece of knowledge that is, in general, unobtainable. I know Timmy eats his vegetables; I know this tends to make him healthy; so far so good. What I do not know is that a mountain lion will eat him on the way to school. Prediction must quantify over an open future full of possible defeaters, and you can never fully discharge that burden in advance. Explanation faces no such problem, because it runs backward from a completed fact. If a stronger mechanism operated, it has *already* left its mark in the very effect to be explained. Timmy's half-devoured corpse, the lion fleeing the scene—the overriding cause is handed to you as data. You did not have to foresee it; you only have to read it off. That is the whole asymmetry: prediction must anticipate every interferer, explanation need only account for the ones that visibly fired. It is the asymmetry between an open system and a closed one, and it falls out of the plain logic of causation in time. An inductivist would grant it, an instrumentalist would grant it, a brute with good intuitions and no philosophy would grant it.

Now Popper. His asymmetry is a different animal entirely, and it does not touch mine. For Popper the difference is that a *satisfactory explanation* must deduce the explanandum from laws whose explanans is independently testable—deeper than the thing explained, checkable by tests other than the one it was summoned to cover, ideally better corroborated—whereas prediction carries no such requirement. So his asymmetry lives not in time, not in the availability of defeater-information, but in an extra logical demand he places on good explanation: independent testability. It is an artifact of his apparatus—corroboration, depth, anti-inductivism—and it delivers an asymmetry only to someone who has already bought that apparatus.

And that is why Popper's analysis is garbage. Not because it is false in its own terms, but because it is parasitic and evasive. It reaches past the obvious, theory-neutral asymmetry sitting right in front of him—the open-versus-closed-system point—in order to route the whole matter through the concepts his system needed to look load-bearing. He preferred the answer that flatters his machinery to the better answer that does not need it. Worse, his analysis is built on a borrowed error he never questioned. The symmetry thesis he is amending—Hempel's—assumes that explanation and prediction are both *deductions from covering laws*, differing only in timing. Popper swallowed that premise whole and merely bolted an extra clause onto the explanation side. But the premise is false. The unscientific brute explains Timmy's death (lion) and predicts that the corpse will soon stink, and neither performance is a deduction from any explicitly formulated law. He has written down no law of predation, no law of putrefaction, no initial conditions, no rule of inference. He simply grasps that the lion's eating Timmy *brought about* the death, and that the body's chemistry *will bring about* the stink. Both acts are competent, veridical, legitimate—and both bypass the covering-law apparatus entirely. So explanation and prediction are not species of law-deduction at all. They are species of causal grasp, and the laws, where they appear, are summaries of causal regularities that license the inference, not the thing the inference consists in.

The verdict, then, is not merely that Popper missed the simpler asymmetry. It is that he conceded Hempel's founding mistake—explanation equals law-deduction—and then spent his ingenuity patching a model malformed at the root. Both men mistook a costume for the body. The brute, with good intuitions and no covering laws, is doing real science's real work. The deductive-nomological model is the suit they hung on him afterward and then argued about as though it were his skeleton.
