What These Authors Are Doing
There is a single procedure at work in Falvey on self-knowledge and in Edgington on conditionals, vagueness, and the preface paradox, and it is worth naming plainly. The author takes an entrenched position from the professional literature and treats it as a fixed datum: for Falvey, content externalism; for Edgington, the identification of a conditional's value with a conditional probability, and more broadly the reduction of every semantic standing to a number. The datum is then held immovable. When it collides with something obvious and far more certain than itself — that a person knows what he is thinking about, that "if Smith is a whale then Smith is a mammal" is true, that an author holds different kinds of commitment toward different sentences in his book — the collision is not treated as a refutation of the datum. It is treated as a problem to be managed. The author protects the entrenched premise and sacrifices the obvious fact, then constructs technical apparatus to make the sacrifice appear principled. The correct inference in every case was the reverse: when a specialized doctrine entails an absurdity, the absurdity discredits the doctrine.
The apparatus is not incidental to this; it is the mechanism. A plain observation — that introspection is not perception, that a statement is not a number, that authorial belief is heterogeneous — credentials no one, because anyone can see it and it requires no expertise to state. A formal construction credentials its author: it can be published, defended, taught, and cited, and it certifies him as rigorous. So the obvious fact must first be flattened into something a machine can process, because only then does the machine become necessary, and only the machine yields the professional recognition the activity exists to secure. The damage to the subject matter is therefore not an unfortunate byproduct but a precondition: vast tracts of perfectly sound understanding are paved over precisely so that an unneeded structure can be erected on the cleared ground. The work is not the pursuit of truth conducted with unusual care. It is the pursuit of recognition, conducted by destroying whatever true and obvious thing stands where the apparatus is to be built.
