# The Barracuda Could Never Happen

There is a world where being right buys you rank. It is not philosophy. It is *RoboCop*.

Watch the structure. The Old Man runs OCP. Dick Jones is upper-middle management — the scumbag who feels he has *earned* his place, tenured in all but name, seniority and resume and a chair at the table. Bob Morton is the little barracuda: an unranked punk who skipped the line. And the Old Man backs Morton over Jones. Why? Because Morton delivered. RoboCop works. Jones's ED-209 shot a man to death in the boardroom. Performance bought standing. The barracuda gets the win and the senior man gets humiliated, because in that world results convert into rank. There is a currency, and the currency is backed by something real.

None of that happens in philosophy. Ever. Not once. In philosophy there is exactly one operative fact: who has what rank. Everything else is decoration on top of that fact.

Run the same cast through a philosophy department and watch it die. The Jones–Old Man disagreements would be ceremonial. Two tenured men basking — rubbing their calling cards together, performing a "foundational dispute" that determines nobody's funding, nobody's prestige, nobody's anything. Signifying status, not content. That part survives, because it costs nothing.

But the Morton–Old Man alignment — the unranked guy who is actually *right* getting rewarded for being right — does not survive. It is not even ceremonial. It is a category error. Worth in a court cannot enter from below on the strength of mere correctness. So a barracuda who delivers is not a rising star. He is a serial killer who happens to be very good at tennis. The talent is real and completely irrelevant to admission, because admission runs on authorization, not on function. There is no mechanism by which "his thing worked" turns into "give him rank." The Old Man would not have backed him. The Old Man would have stared at his feet and walked away.

Now here is the part that really finishes it.

You'd think the closest philosophy could get to this would be theft. Jones can't *reward* Morton — but surely Jones could rip him off. Take the working product, slap his own name on it, take the win. That's the cynical move, and in any normal corrupt institution it happens hourly.

It wouldn't happen. Not cleanly. Because clean theft of a *good* idea is a confession. To steal Morton's thing whole, Jones first has to recognize it as right and valuable — and acting on that recognition admits the one lethal premise the entire court exists to suppress: that being right is what mattered. A faithful copy preserves the evidence that the outsider was correct and the insider wasn't. So wholehearted plagiarism is off the table. Not for ethical reasons. For structural ones. It ratifies the outsider's standard, and ratifying that standard is suicide.

What actually happens — and I have watched it happen — is the only thing that *can* happen. The point gets taken, but garbled. Half-absorbed. Stripped of the exact part that made it sharp. Reattributed to the fold, smeared across two or three insiders so no single act of theft is visible and no clean line points back to the non-person who had it first. Lackluster, partial, mangled plagiarism. And the mangling is not sloppiness. It is the mechanism. The idea *has* to be degraded, because a faithful copy would indict them. Wrecking the idea is how the court launders it — it converts an unauthorized correct claim into a vague, authorized, collectively-held murmur that traces back to no one and therefore indicts no one. The damage to the idea is the price of admission.

So measure the distance. In the OCP world, the barracuda's correctness is convertible — it buys him the office, and his rival, to survive, would gladly steal it whole. In the philosophy world, the barracuda's correctness buys nothing, and his rival won't even fully steal it, because stealing it whole would mean admitting it was worth stealing.

That is the whole thing in one image. Jones rips off Ferrer with conviction. That is a world with a currency. The philosopher can't even bring himself to plagiarize wholeheartedly — he can only garble. That is a world with none. There is no nectar. There is just the door, and the people standing in front of it making sure being right never gets you through it.
