# Provenances, Not Propositions

"The moon is a giant ant" is perfectly falsifiable. Go look; check for legs, for chitin, for the absence of three thousand kilometers of rock. It fails the test instantly. Yet no one would call it scientific. So falsifiability cannot be what makes a claim scientific, because the property is satisfied in heaps by claims that are not science at all—jokes, whims, things pulled from the air. At most it is necessary. It is nowhere near sufficient.

What disqualifies the ant-moon is not anything in the sentence. It is the sentence's provenance: it was not arrived at by any consideration of empirical data, it was simply asserted. And this points to the correction Popper could never make, because his whole program was built to forbid it: it is not contentions that are scientific or unscientific, but the ways they are arrived at. Scientific is a predicate of the route, not the proposition. The identical sentence could be a lunatic's guess or, in some strange counterfactual, the end of a careful inferential chain—unscientific in the first case, scientific in the second, with the content fixed throughout. Popper wanted a property of statements to carry a burden that only a property of procedures can carry. That is the category error at the root of the thing.

So what makes a provenance scientific? Not mere empiricism. Every time a three-year-old uses his eyes he is being empirical, and he is not being scientific. Not mere scalability either, since using one's eyes is scalable. The differentia is this: a scientific provenance considers empirical data by methods that are both truth-conducive and intersubjectively adjudicable—and adjudicable in the strong sense, not the thin one. The toddler's looking is checkable only in that we can both look; it has no machinery for catching the illusion or the confound that both of us would get wrong together. A scientific method is built to expose and correct its own errors: controlled, repeatable, able to discriminate a hypothesis from its rivals, so that disagreement can be driven to resolution rather than left to taste. Truth-conduciveness is not a separate axiom sitting alongside this; it is what error-correcting adjudication reliably produces over time. A method earns the name "truth-conducive" precisely because it is built to be policed. Bare empiricism lacks the policing, and so does bare falsifiability. Neither the toddler nor the ant-moon is doing science, and for the same reason: there is no self-correcting adjudication in either. That, and not the logical shape of a sentence, is where scientificity lives.
