# Marx Is Wrong; Jung Is Empty

It is a higher status to be wrong than to be empty. Marx earns the first; Jung does not rise even to that. This is the opposite of the received view, on which Marx is the unfalsifiable pseudo-scientist (so Popper alleges) and Jung is merely soft or speculative. The truth is the reverse. Marx made a clear, contentful, refutable claim and was refuted. Jung made no refutable claim at all.

Take Marx on his own terms. The motor of history is class antagonism: the underclass hates the overclass and moves against it, and the overclass is correspondingly defensive and treacherous toward the underclass. The categories are monolithic—"proletariat," "bourgeoisie"—and the strokes are broad. But broad is not meaningless. The blocks are intelligible blocks. You know exactly what the theory predicts and exactly what would contradict it. It predicts that worker Fred, structurally pitted against foreman Bob, resents Bob and acts against him. And here is what actually happens: Fred butters Bob up, angles for the promotion, and admires the very man he is supposedly fated to overthrow. When Fred turns violent, he does not kill Bob; he kills other Freds—often precisely in order to climb out of the Fred class and become a Bob. The prediction is wrong, and it is wrong *observably*, which is the whole point. The 1980s settled it in miniature: the blue-collar kids revered Trump the mogul, and the upper-class kids sneered at him, sneered at him *for* his blue-collar vulgarity—a rich man with a poor man's manners. That is the exact inverse of what class-antagonism predicts, and anyone could see it. I have never once heard a poor man talk about eating the rich. That talk comes, without exception, from the upper class. The poor do not want to destroy the rich; they want to suck up to them, and to become them. When I was poor I got nothing but grief from other poors. When I became rich, the plumber and the handyman began treating me as a grandee, a beneficent caretaker—a reverence I had not earned and would not previously have received.

So Marx is false. But false is an achievement. To be false a statement must first *say something*—must carve the possibilities into the ones that confirm it and the ones that kill it. Marx did that. He bet on a specific dynamic, the dynamic did not occur, and the theory lies dead on the field where empirical theories go to die. Popper had it backwards: the problem with Marxism is not that it cannot be refuted, but that it *was* refuted and the Marxists refuse to sign the certificate. The refutations are everywhere. The Marxists simply will not look.

Jung never gets onto that field. His block-categories destroy resolution exactly as Marx's do, but he adds the fatal move Marx never made: he personifies the blocks. And personification does not merely coarsen a claim—it voids its content. Ask what would falsify "you are projecting your anima," or "that was the shadow." There is no answer. A posited inner person can be credited with wanting anything; "leaking" can take any form—the behavior, its absence, its opposite, its sublimation. Every outcome confirms. Marx at least said the proletariat would *do a specific thing*. Jung says there is a feminine figure in your psyche, and no observation whatever turns that into a prediction that could come out wrong. Marx is falsifiable and false. Jung is neither true nor false, because he is not saying anything that could be one or the other. He is unintelligible in the strict sense: not hard to understand, but lacking the determinate content that understanding would latch onto. Marx got the world wrong. Jung never made contact with it. And being wrong about the world is a relationship with the world—which is more than Jung ever had.
