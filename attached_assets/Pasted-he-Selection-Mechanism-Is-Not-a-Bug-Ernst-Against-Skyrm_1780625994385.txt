he Selection Mechanism Is Not a Bug: Ernst Against Skyrms
Brian Skyrms argues that the social contract — the emergence of fairness, cooperation, and coordinated reciprocity among self-interested agents — can be explained by the tools of evolutionary game theory, without a designer or a rational covenant. Populations of agents play games, strategies are subject to evolutionary dynamics, and over time viable cooperative equilibria emerge. Against this, Ernst objects that the models exhibit a "robustness failure": the outcome is highly sensitive to fine-grained modeling choices — the update rule, the spatial or social arrangement of agents, the level of noise or mutation, the timing of interactions. Change these settings and the cooperative result can vanish or flip. Since the settings are chosen largely for tractability rather than known to be true, Ernst concludes that the models do not robustly establish that the social contract would evolve, and that this sensitivity complicates Skyrms's project.
The objection misfires, and it misfires because it has misread the modal force of Skyrms's claim. Ernst's complaint would land only if Skyrms were committed to the thesis that the cooperative equilibrium emerges across most or all parameter settings — that the social contract is a near-inevitable attractor whatever the conditions. But Skyrms is committed to no such thing, and an evolutionary explanation positively requires the opposite. The claim is selective, not universal: it is that the social contract is downstream of evolutionary winnowing. Most population structures, under most dynamics, produce poor or fragile equilibria, and the populations stuck with those equilibria fragment, are outcompeted, or are destroyed. The cooperative order we actually observe is what came through the filter — the residue of the runs that survived. Sensitivity to conditions is not a defect in this story. It is the engine of it. A process of selection presupposes that different conditions yield different outcomes and that most outcomes lose; without that variation there would be nothing for selection to act upon and no explanation to give.
So the very fact Ernst brandishes as a refutation is a premise the theory needs. The collectives rotated out of existence by their own defective equilibria are not counterexamples to Skyrms; they are confirming instances. They show precisely what the selective account asserts — that equilibria have differential survival value and that the bad ones are eliminated, leaving the survivors sitting on the equilibrium that works, which is later reframed as the "social contract." To point at the failed populations as though they wounded the theory is to mistake the discards the theory presupposes for evidence against it. It is the dodo objection to natural selection: confronted with the claim that surviving organisms are arranged so as to breathe and not foul themselves, one does not refute it by observing that extinct species also breathed. The extinct are what selection selected against; their corpses are evidence for the mechanism, not against it.
The underlying error is a single recurring one. Ernst reads the model as a static deductive object — does cooperation come out robustly across the parameter space? — when it is a description of a temporal, eliminative process: which equilibria persist once the non-viable ones are removed? Freeze the dynamics into a still photograph and the parameter-sensitivity looks like a flaw; restore the motion and the eliminative character that the formalism was built to capture, and the sensitivity is exactly the mechanism doing its work. The right equilibrium is, by definition, what survives — and that is not a hidden assumption Skyrms must defend but the entire content of an evolutionary explanation. Ernst found the load-bearing beam of the theory and filed a complaint against it as though it were a crack. The negative half of his paper required handling the apparatus; it did not require understanding what the apparatus was a model of, and the difference is the whole of the mistake.
